# Paths owns breadth; Trippo should own trust

**Paths' weakest surface is the one Trippo built best.** The only two substantive accuracy complaints in Paths' entire public review corpus are grounding failures — it answered a request for places to stay near Narita Airport with Mount Fuji, roughly 190 km off ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ai.gopaths.pitravel)), and it ignored a Singapore user's explicit instruction to cut museums, who concluded "Compared to ChatGPT, the output is not as detailed and good" ([App Store SG](https://apps.apple.com/sg/app/id6786985803)). Neither is a parsing bug; both are the generative planner inventing things it was not entitled to say. Trippo's planner is architecturally incapable of that specific failure: the model returns citation tags against a list the server sent, `hydrate()` rebuilds each block from the stored place record, and an item that does not resolve against Trippo's own data is not attributed on screen. That is the asset. It is also, bluntly, the only asset in Trippo that Paths cannot buy with three more weeks of shipping — Paths has shipped **18 versions in roughly 13 weeks** ([App Store version history](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/paths-trip-itinerary-agent/id6786985803)), carries **10K+ Android downloads** ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ai.gopaths.pitravel)), and markets the identical "link, screenshot, or text → itinerary" wedge Trippo is built on. Worse for Trippo's current thesis, unredacted WHOIS on `gopaths.ai` lists a registrant email at **`hcmp-admin@xiaohongshu.com`**, and Xiaohongshu's own AI assistant 点点 shipped an Itinerary Mode in 2026 that can read a user's own saved notes as context ([百度百科: 点点](https://baike.baidu.com/item/%E7%82%B9%E7%82%B9/65296356)). The honest conclusion is that link extraction is on its way to being a platform feature, not a moat — so Trippo should keep extraction as its on-ramp, stop selling it as the product, and sell the thing the whole category is failing at instead: a plan where every item resolves to a real place, says where it came from, and can be edited by hand.

Here is what is *not* evidenced, and the researchers are emphatic about it. **No acquisition, investment, or corporate relationship between Xiaohongshu (or 行吟信息科技) and 超超世世, or between either and BLANK CRAFT, was found in any outlet** — 36氪, 虎嗅, 晚点, TechNode, KrASIA and targeted Chinese searches all returned nothing on point. The strategic implication survives the uncertainty, which is why this matters. If the RED link is real, Trippo's current thesis puts it in direct competition with the rights-holder of the content it parses — an opponent that can extract losslessly and lawfully because it owns the corpus, that already imports a user's own liked and saved notes as context in 点点 on PC since 29 May 2026 ([品玩](https://www.pingwest.com/a/301644)), that has shipped an Itinerary Mode, and that is investing commercially in travel with **39,000+ professional travel accounts and 21 million+ new travel notes monthly** on its own figures ([中国日报网](https://cn.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202604/27/WS69ef1038a310942cc49a98ab.html)). If the RED link is *not* real — if Paths is the 圆周旅迹 team, or an unrelated team, shipping internationally through a Singapore shell — then Trippo faces a fast, well-staffed, China-trained competitor with a mature internal platform and a battle-tested parser, already live in Malaysia and Singapore, and still has to contend with 点点 行程模式 separately, because that ships regardless of who owns Paths. **Under both branches the conclusion is identical: do not build a company whose defensibility rests on being good at reading RED links.** The branches differ only in how fast the window closes, not in which direction.

## The category's graveyard says planning does not pay, and the giants know it

The Western AI-planner field has already consolidated and is thinly capitalised outside two names. **Expedia acquired Layla on 31 July 2026** for undisclosed terms after Layla raised only ~€5M, having claimed 5M users and $1B in planned trip value ([Expedia IR](https://ir.expediagroup.com/news-and-events/news/news-details/2026/Expedia-Group-acquires-Layla-accelerating-its-AI-powered-trip-planning-and-booking-strategy/default.aspx); [Skift](https://skift.com/2026/07/31/expedia-acquired-ai-trip-planner-layla-exclusive/)); Layla had itself bought Roam Around in February 2024 ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/startup-travel-planner-layla-acquires-ai-itinerary-builder-roam-around)). **Mindtrip** is the only well-funded independent at **$22.5M** from Forerunner, Costanoa, Capital One Ventures, United Airlines Ventures and Amex Ventures ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/mindtrip-ai-travel-planning-capital-one-ventures-united-airlines-ventures)), and it is the one direct competitor on Trippo's wedge: its "Start Anywhere" converts "a link, screenshot, or social post" into an itinerary ([TeachUsTechnology](https://teachustechnology.com/mindtrip-ai-trip-planner-with-in-chat-booking/)). Tripnotes shut down in December 2023, Google Trips died in 2019, and Skift's verdict on the pattern is unsparing: superior UX alone gave Hipmunk — "the best-loved interface in travel search" — no structural advantage before SAP Concur switched it off, and "the graveyard of travel startups is full of itinerary builders" ([Skift, 30 July 2026](https://skift.com/2026/07/30/dont-start-a-travel-startup/)). Pricing has converged hard: **$39.99–$49.99/year** is the ceiling (Wanderlog Pro $39.99, Layla $49.99, Stippl €24.99, TripIt Pro $49), and the paywalled features are identical everywhere — offline access, unlimited AI, multi-user editing, export.

The incumbents are more capable and less committed than their marketing suggests. **Gemini now builds routed, day-by-day itineraries grounded in Google Maps, Hotels and Flights, incorporating YouTube creator content, and personalised from Gmail confirmations and past bookings** ([Google Blog, 6 Aug 2026](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/how-gemini-plans-trips/)) — plan generation is a free commodity and Trippo must not compete on it. Google Maps has shipped Gemini-powered **"Screenshots"**, reading places out of screenshot text and plotting them on a shareable list, since March 2025 ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/03/27/google-maps-gemini-screenshots/)). Yet **Google's output has no durable trip object**: Canvas itineraries launched desktop-only, US-only, behind a Labs opt-in ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-extends-ai-travel-planning-and-agentic-booking-in-search/561251/)), Maps lists are flat, and Gemini chats are ephemeral. **Expedia deprioritised its Romie concierge after concluding the end-to-end concept "wasn't practical"**, pivoting to specialised agents; **Chesky has pushed Airbnb's real agent to 2027** and described it as "much richer than a chatbot", a visual interface rather than a chat window ([Skift](https://skift.com/2026/07/31/expedia-acquired-ai-trip-planner-layla-exclusive/); [The AI Insider](https://theaiinsider.tech/2026/10/01/airbnb-introduces-ai-search-as-brian-chesky-bets-on-cautious-conversion-focused-rollout/)). Two incumbents independently concluded chat is the wrong container and neither has shipped the replacement. **Tripadvisor** has pushed its billion-review corpus outward into Claude and Alexa+ rather than deepening its own app ([Tripadvisor Newsroom](https://tripadvisor.mediaroom.com/2026-04-23-How-AI-Is-Changing-the-Way-We-Plan-Travel)). **TripIt** validates the trip container as a paid product at $49/year and has added extraction, not generation — its new "Image to Plan" runs on **Apple Intelligence's on-device foundation models** to pull dates, times, locations and confirmation numbers out of photos and PDFs ([SAP Concur](https://www.concur.com/blog/article/sap-showcases-new-ai-integrated-travel-and-expense-enhancements-and-global)). **Apple ships no planner at all**, offering only multi-stop routing up to 15 stops and iOS 26's Visited Places — a vacated position and, via on-device models, an enabler.

The reason the incumbents under-invest is visible in their own numbers. **Less than 1% of Booking Holdings room nights originate from AI channels**, AI usage collapses from ~30% at inspiration to roughly **6% at booking**, and **only 2% of leisure travellers will let AI book for them**, while **51% distrust AI's reliability** ([Skift, 11 Oct 2026](https://skift.com/2026/10/11/travel-ais-prove-it-phase-visibility-and-the-booking-gap/); [Skift State of Travel 2026](https://skift.com/insights/state-of-travel/)). Planning is a marketing surface for them, so their roadmaps bend toward conversion rather than planning quality — and features that cost engineering while generating no bookings (deep editing, offline, collaboration) are unlikely to arrive. The best-sourced structural gap follows from the same pipeline: analysis of 27,360 AI travel answers across 659 Barcelona properties found **independent hotels are 8x more likely to be unknown to AI systems than chains**, with recommendation rates roughly equal once known — "the gap is discovery, not favoritism" — and Google review volume was the strongest factor in discoverability ([Skift](https://skift.com/2026/10/11/travel-ais-prove-it-phase-visibility-and-the-booking-gap/)). Independent, low-review-volume venues are precisely what creator posts document and generic planners cannot see.

The Chinese extraction category is the one place this has been proven at scale, and its lesson is double-edged. 圆周旅迹 grew on 抄作业 — copying a specific creator's homework — and Chinese trade press reports that **over 80% of its users cite the copy/extraction function as crucial versus ~75% for the AI planning**, with the nickname 邪修 for the unorthodox-but-effective build ([环球旅讯](https://m.traveldaily.cn/article/188945)). Extraction out-polls generation, which validates the wedge. But the same source reports the product is **entirely free with no IAP after a year-plus and ~10M registered users**, with the team eyeing brand partnerships and bespoke tours rather than subscriptions, and explicitly declining to push booking conversion. The category has enormous top-of-funnel pull and **no proven direct monetisation**. Treat the 10M figure with care: it comes from a single outlet, sourced to the team, in a piece referencing the October 2024 Golden Week — it is 2024–2025 vintage. Equally, every user-sentiment finding on 圆周旅迹 in the notes is positive, and the researchers state plainly that this is a sampling artefact of vendor-adjacent write-ups and promotional RED notes, not a verified read on satisfaction.

Southeast Asia, finally, is under-sourced in this research and should be caveated. No Tech in Asia, e27 or Vulcan Post coverage of social-extraction travel startups was found; no download, MAU or funding figures exist for the regional AI planners named (Trevia, Stippl, Visit77), several of which may be pre-traction websites. What is sourced: **Xiaohongshu, at 300M+ MAU, is already the planning layer for inbound Chinese travel to SEA** ([Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2024/xiaohongshu-southeast-asia-tourism/)); incumbents defend transactions (Traveloka, HQ'd in Singapore, now lets customers rebook itineraries in natural language) and daily mobility (Grab); and Traveloka's Q3 2026 index shows heavy **intra-regional, multi-city** demand ([PR Newswire APAC](https://www.prnewswire.com/apac/news-releases/traveloka-sea-index-q3-2026-faith-and-heritage-stand-out-across-southeast-asia-302906874.html)), which is exactly where sequencing pays off most.

## What to build, in order: make the plan verifiably true, then make it legible

The ordering principle is that every item either closes a documented failure Paths exhibits, removes a documented reason users churn, or exploits an asset Trippo already has. Nothing here requires a team.

| Tier | Build | Evidence it rests on |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — before any release** | Kill the `needs_info` gate: always render the draft, demote questions to optional refinements on the plan | A generated plan "arrives un-owned"; first-run quality is decisive when acquisition is influencer-seeded; Paths' SEA ratings show how fast a tiny base turns negative |
| **1** | Inline travel-time connector row between blocks, plus a per-day total | Named the highest-value single component; Wanderlog renders both; Paths' own 5★ praise cites exactly this |
| **1** | A map sheet under the day list — bottom sheet over a map, row selection highlights the pin and back | Map in 14 of 20 apps; Paths' two detailed positives both name the map; one surface at a time with a draggable sheet is the convention users already know |
| **1** | Make verification visible: "resolved against your source" state on the card, publication-named provenance, held-back items shown as held back | NN/g: style citations distinctly, place them adjacent to the claim, name the publication, and do the verification for the user rather than showing a link; Trippo already stores `from_place_id` and `from_source_title` |
| **1** | Sign-in hygiene: no forced account for a first trip, Apple sign-in without email, working cross-device sync | Three of four non-accuracy Paths complaints are account-related, including a 1★ churn over email privacy and two independent requests for cross-device sync |
| **2 — next** | Screenshot/OCR ingestion replacing the `needs_manual` paste, on-device where possible | Paths claims it, Trippo lacks it; **57.3% of places in travel TikToks are not named in the caption** and must be recovered from on-screen text, audio or comments ([Dreamfare](https://dreamfare.app/guides/what-people-save-from-travel-tiktoks)); materially lower legal exposure than server-side fetching; on-device models cut Gemini quota pressure, following TripIt's Apple Intelligence precedent |
| **2** | Restore reordering as a **tap-only menu first** (Move up / Move down / Move to day… / Unschedule), drag as the optional twin | ~49% of phone use is one-handed and ~75% of interactions thumb-driven (Hoober 2013 — dated, directional only); drag has no accessible equivalent on its own; Wanderlog ships both |
| **2** | Promote the references screen into a first-class **unscheduled/wishlist bucket**; assigning a day becomes a later cheap action | Travellers spend ~5 hours with travel content in the 45 days before booking while dates arrive late; saving must never require a date; Wanderlog's "leave unscheduled" is the reference. Trippo is one screen away from this already |
| **2** | Per-day refinement instead of whole-plan resend | NN/g's compartmentalisation and point-to-select fixes for "apple picking"; Paths' own chip is scoped to Day 2; also cuts token spend on a free tier |
| **3 — after release signal** | Rail/transit mode in travel times for KL, SG and BKK | A Malaysian reviewer asked Paths for exactly this; all three test cities are rail cities |
| **3** | In-trip "next 90 minutes" view during trip dates, using the existing date envelope | The in-trip job is answering what's next, not reviewing the plan; the most common AI failures found in-destination are wrong prices or hours (27%) and unavailability (21%) — flagged as a single-vendor survey, methodology unstated |
| **3** | Lightweight per-item reactions before any co-editing | Group planning derails on "unclear enthusiasm levels"; the shape is one editor and N reactors, not N co-editors |

**What not to build, and why.** *Booking and affiliate*: it needs inventory contracts (Sabre, Priceline, Viator) and payment liability a solo developer cannot assume, and the demand is not there — under 1% of Booking's room nights come from AI, 2% of travellers will let AI book, 58% say they would not book a trip through TikTok or Instagram, and 圆周旅迹 declined booking monetisation at ~10M users. Being deliberately booking-agnostic also removes the incentive conflict shaping every competitor's UI. *Fare search*: leave `flightSearch` off — the provider is mock, and Google Flight Deals already covers 200+ countries in 60+ languages ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/17/google-rolls-out-its-ai-flight-deals-tool-globally-adds-new-travel-features-in-search/)). *Groups and collaboration*: keep the flag off. Paths markets it hardest and its own reviews show single-user two-device sync failing, which is weak proxy evidence that its real-time layer is unreliable; the group evidence base is qualitative only, with the quotable numbers coming from vendor case studies the researchers explicitly refused to report. Fix one user on two devices before two users on one trip. *A full map workspace* — 3D, live traffic, draggable routes, offline tiles: 圆周旅迹 already has these and a solo developer cannot win there. *Offline mode*: a precondition rather than a driver of opens, and a commodity paywall line everywhere; defer. *A web app, voice input, a community/找搭子 feed, a separate Fact Check tool, and checklist/notes/photos/weather modules*: breadth Paths has and Trippo does not need to match, because none of it addresses why a plan is trustworthy. *Chat as the primary surface*: Expedia abandoned it, Chesky rejected it, and NN/g's accordion-editing and apple-picking findings explain why — keep Refine as a tab beside a directly manipulable plan, never as the only way to change something.

**Defensible versus commoditised.** Commoditised now: prose plan generation (free in Gemini, grounded in Maps/Flights/Hotels and personalised from Gmail), route optimisation (Wanderlog gates it at $39.99/yr), weather, checklists, offline, unlimited AI, and multi-user editing — the standard paid tier everywhere. Becoming commoditised: link and screenshot extraction, held today by Mindtrip at $22.5M, by Paths, and by Google Maps' Gemini Screenshots. Genuinely defensible for one developer: the **resolution discipline** (nothing rendered or attributed unless it resolves against stored data) and the provenance UI built on it, because it is an architectural commitment competitors would have to retrofit against their own speed incentives; the **flight-derived date envelope**, which no app in this entire landscape was found to have and which turns a real constraint into visible honesty ("Day 1 runs from 6:10 PM, not the morning"); and **local ground truth for independent SEA venues**, which attacks the best-quantified gap in the research — the 8x invisibility of independents, driven by Google review volume, which is exactly the class of place a RED or creator post names and a generic planner cannot see.

**The legal position, stated plainly.** RED's enforcement record is real and successful. It reported blocking **15.96 billion crawler attacks in three months** and sued multiple companies in July 2022 ([央广网](http://tech.cnr.cn/techph/20220708/t20220708_525904562.shtml)). A Changzhou company and its employees were criminally prosecuted for cracking the app's encryption and forging data requests, netting **RMB 6.53M+ between June 2019 and December 2021**; the company was fined RMB 200,000 and **three employees received prison terms from three years (suspended five) to four years** ([安全内参](https://www.secrss.com/articles/72097)). In a civil case, a company that crawled RED data for profit lost on final appeal and was ordered to pay **RMB 4.9 million** ([网易](https://m.163.com/dy/article/JU5N3CRA05129QAF.html)), with courts holding that rotating user IDs and accelerating IP rotation to bypass technical protective measures constituted infringement ([天同律师事务所](https://www.tiantonglaw.com/Content/2024/06-27/1022448210.html)). The aggravating factors the courts named — commercial purpose, systematic operation, circumvention of technical measures — are all present in any server-side extraction product that works reliably at scale. Three practical conclusions. First, **do not build infrastructure whose reliability depends on defeating RED's anti-bot measures**: no proxy pools, no ID rotation, no header spoofing, no cached corpus of post bodies beyond what the user's own trip needs. Second, **move ingestion toward screenshot/OCR and on-device parsing**, which carries materially less exposure because the content arrives from the user's device rather than from Trippo's servers, and which is simultaneously the platform-agnostic path and the one that recovers the 57.3% of places that captions omit. Third, **calibrate the risk**: RED's PRC judgments do not bind Malaysian or Singaporean courts, where ToS breach and copyright are the likelier theories, and the researchers could not retrieve RED's current user agreement or robots.txt verbatim — so the characterisation above rests on the litigation record, not on quoted contract terms. The relevant signal is a demonstrated willingness to litigate, and the right posture is to never be the cheapest defendant to make an example of.

## Malaysia and Chinese-language content are the position, not a constraint

Paths is weakest exactly where Trippo lives. Its **2.67 in Singapore, 3.00 in Hong Kong, 3.71 in Malaysia and 3.67 in Taiwan against 4.67 in the US** is the clearest competitive opening in this research, and the complaints driving those numbers are local: no train mode in a region of rail cities, geographic reasoning errors in Asia, no WeChat login for a Chinese-seeded audience. A Malaysian developer who has tested Kuala Lumpur, Singapore and Bangkok, whose app already handles Chinese-language source content, and whose planner refuses to attribute unresolved places, is positioned against those specific failures rather than against Paths' feature count.

The target market follows: **intra-regional, multi-city SEA travel for Chinese- and English-reading travellers** — Malaysian and Singaporean Chinese, Taiwan and Hong Kong outbound, and mainland outbound into SEA, all of whom already plan on RED. This matches the demand shape in the Traveloka index and the observation that AI planning pays off most on multi-city trips, and it matches where RED's content is densest for SEA destinations. Language order should be English first (regional lingua franca, and the Malaysian reviewer who waited for an English version before installing Paths is direct evidence of the funnel leak a Chinese-first listing creates), then Simplified and Traditional Chinese, with Bahasa Melayu and Thai later — noting that this ordering is the researchers' inference from demand corridors, not a sourced finding, and that no data on SEA willingness to pay for planning tools was found at all.

On pricing, stay free through launch. The strongest positive review Paths has in Malaysia is a user delighted that a subscription did not exist, against a reference ceiling of $39.99–$49.99/year set by venture-subsidised competitors and a Chinese incumbent that could not monetise at ~10M users. Do not build subscription plumbing before there is retention to charge for; if monetisation becomes necessary, pay-per-trip (Lambus €9.99, TripProf from €3.99) is the only pattern in the research that does not require out-competing a free tier.

## The honest answer: the thesis holds as a mechanism, not as a position

Trippo's core thesis — read the links the user already found and sequence them — is correct about user behaviour and wrong as a defensible position. The behavioural half is well evidenced: extraction out-polls generation among Chinese users at 80% versus 75%, the trust in a copied itinerary comes from an identifiable peer who actually went, and the structural pain (no geocoding, no sequencing, no time budgeting, no shared editing in feed-optimised content) will not be fixed by better models. But the mechanism is being absorbed from three directions at once. RED's 点点 has an Itinerary Mode and can use the user's own saved notes as context, which is the structurally lossless and lawful version of "copy that note's trip". Google Maps reads places out of screenshots. Mindtrip sells "Start Anywhere" as a headline differentiator on $22.5M. And Paths — possibly RED-adjacent, certainly fast — ships the same claim weekly in Trippo's home market.

So the thesis needs to shift, and the shift is narrow rather than wholesale. **Stop positioning on where the places come from; position on what happens to them after they arrive.** Extraction becomes the on-ramp, mentioned in onboarding and never in the pitch. The pitch becomes the thing every competitor in the research is documented failing at: a plan where nothing appears unless it resolved to a real place, where every item says which post it came from and lets you go back and check, where the plan is an object you edit by hand rather than a chat transcript you re-prompt, and where the app tells you when a day will not physically work — including when your own flight has eaten the morning. That claim is true of Trippo today in a way it is not true of anything else in this research, it is grounded in the best-evidenced user problem (**one in six AI-using travellers was recommended a place that does not exist**, and among those who hit a problem 21% lost trust versus 5% of those who did not — single-vendor survey, syndicated, methodology unstated, so directional), and it points engineering at `hydrate()`, the connector row, the provenance chip and the map sheet rather than at a parser arms race against the platform that owns the content. The parser should be good enough to be invisible. It should not be the story.

## Open questions only the developer can resolve

**Install Paths and run three trips through it.** That single action settles more open questions than any further desk research: whether a 小红书 link actually parses outside China, whether Instagram and TikTok links parse, how screenshot extraction performs against the same post, whether a Share Sheet extension exists, what the onboarding sequence and permission prompts are, whether there is a tab bar, whether items drag or use a menu, whether the agent edits in place or regenerates the day, whether a free-tier generation cap exists, and whether a KL or Penang post resolves to real local venues or to tourist-trail chains. Every one of those is marked unverified in the research, several because the App Store screenshots are composites and no teardown exists anywhere. Run the same KL post that produced 7 of 7 cited blocks in Trippo and compare directly.

Four others remain, in descending order of consequence. **Does Paths' parser hold up on independent Malaysian venues?** — if it inherits the 8x independent-invisibility problem, that is the opening; if it does not, the local-ground-truth strategy needs rethinking. **What is the actual Gemini cost per trip at the planned output ceiling**, and does the per-day refinement change in Tier 2 bring it inside free-tier quota — because the roadmap above assumes it does. **Is a Trippo trip usable on a second device today**, given that this is the failure Paths' own users reported twice and the precondition for ever turning the groups flag on. And **what is the smallest map that satisfies the feasibility question** — the recommendation is a bottom sheet over tiles with pin/row linkage, but whether that is a week or a month of work for one developer in Flutter determines whether it is Tier 1 or Tier 2, and only the developer knows.

## Conclusion

The most useful thing this research shows is that the extraction wedge and the trust gap are not the same opportunity, and Trippo has accidentally built the harder one. Everyone is racing to parse more sources; nobody is competing on whether the resulting plan is true. Paths, Mindtrip, Gemini and 点点 will all converge on reading any link or screenshot a user hands them, because that is a model capability and model capabilities arrive on a schedule nobody controls. What does not arrive on a schedule is the discipline to refuse to render an item that failed to resolve, and the engineering to rebuild a plan from data you already hold rather than from what the model claimed. That is a design decision Trippo has already made and that its best-funded competitors are structurally disinclined to make, because their incentive is speed and breadth and, for the incumbents, booking conversion that planning quality does not improve.

The window is narrower than the asset is durable. Paths is shipping weekly into Malaysia and Singapore, Airbnb's real agent lands in 2027, and RED's Itinerary Mode is already live domestically. A solo developer cannot out-ship any of them, which is the argument for a deliberately small first release: a verified, provenance-bearing, hand-editable plan with travel times, a map sheet, and honest short days — and visibly none of the breadth. The failure mode to avoid is the one the graveyard documents. Hipmunk had the best interface in travel search and was switched off anyway, because craft without something the user returns for is not a moat. For Trippo, the thing worth returning for is not that it read your post. It is that it never told you about a place that was not there.
