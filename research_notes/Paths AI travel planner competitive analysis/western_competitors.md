# Western consumer AI travel-planning apps — competitive profiles (current as of 17 October 2026)

Scope note: this file covers Mindtrip, Layla, Wanderboat, Wonderplan, Trip Planner AI, Vacay, GuideGeek, Roam Around, Curiosio, plus adjacent incumbents that set the conventions (Wanderlog, Stippl, Stardrift, iPlan.ai, TripIt, Google Travel). Many price points and UI descriptions come from hands-on review roundups rather than vendor pages — review-roundup sourcing is flagged where it matters, and conflicts are stated rather than resolved silently.

## For each app: core feature set, platforms, launch date, funding raised, user scale

### Takeaway
Only two players in this set are meaningfully capitalised — Mindtrip (~$22.5M, corporate strategics) and Layla (~€5M seed, now owned by Expedia) — and Layla's July 2026 exit removed the strongest independent consumer brand from the field. Everything else is either a bootstrapped free web tool with no disclosed funding, a B2B/white-label business wearing a consumer skin (GuideGeek), or an incumbent itinerary app (Wanderlog) that bolted AI onto an existing map product.

### Inferences

- Layla's exit multiple is instructive: 5M users and $1B in "planned trip value" converted into an undisclosed (and, given ~€5M raised, almost certainly modest) sale. Planning intent at consumer scale was not independently monetisable.

## UI/UX model: chat-first, form-first, or map-first; itinerary presentation and editing; map centrality

### Takeaway
The field splits cleanly into three interaction archetypes — chat-first (Mindtrip, Layla, GuideGeek, Vacay), form-first (Wonderplan, iPlan.ai), and map-first (Wanderlog, Stardrift, Maps GPT) — and almost every AI-native entrant chose chat-first, leaving the map as a supporting panel rather than the workspace. Across the AI-native cohort the itinerary output converges on the same artefact: a vertical day-by-day card list, edited either by talking to the bot again or by light inline tweaks.

### Cited Findings
- **Mindtrip — chat-first.** Conversational search with photos, maps and community itineraries embedded inside the conversation; live Sabre inventory shown and PayPal checkout completed in the chat window; map is "secondary (supportive, not central)"; editing happens by conversational refinement — [travelanywhere hands-on](https://www.travelanywhere.blog/blog/best-agentic-ai-travel-apps-2026-mindtrip-layla-stippl-stardrift-wanderlog-tested)
- Mindtrip described elsewhere as "chat-first with integrated map and live itinerary editor", map centrality "high" — contradicts the hands-on read above — [zplatform roundup](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/); contradicted by [travelanywhere](https://www.travelanywhere.blog/blog/best-agentic-ai-travel-apps-2026-mindtrip-layla-stippl-stardrift-wanderlog-tested)
- Mindtrip collaboration: invited users can "comment, suggest edits, or tag @Mindtrip for AI-balanced suggestions that account for multiple people's preferences at once" — [TeachUsTechnology](https://teachustechnology.com/mindtrip-ai-trip-planner-with-in-chat-booking/)
- **Layla — chat + itinerary hybrid.** Conversational planning producing a day-by-day itinerary view; live flight/hotel/activity/transfer rates shown inside the conversation; "PriceLock" holds rates while deciding; edits stay in-app before handing off to partner checkouts; map present but "less central than Mindtrip" — [travelanywhere](https://www.travelanywhere.blog/blog/best-agentic-ai-travel-apps-2026-mindtrip-layla-stippl-stardrift-wanderlog-tested); [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- **Wanderboat — discovery/POI-first.** Activity and route-centric rather than day-grid-centric; routes displayed but "less workspace-oriented"; positions around "local discovery" and spontaneity over rigid day-by-day structure — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- **Wonderplan — form-first, no chat.** Destination/dates/budget/companions form → editable day-by-day plan; map centrality "low", primarily text with PDF export; explicitly "no chat" — [monkeytravel comparison](https://monkeytravel.app/blog/best-ai-trip-planners-2026-compared); [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- **GuideGeek — chat-only, no workspace.** Lives entirely inside WhatsApp/Instagram/Messenger; map centrality "low"; "no workspace" — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- **Wanderlog — map-first.** Pins, day-grouping, drag-to-reorder for routing; live collaborative sharing; the AI chat is "bolted-on (secondary, not native)" and capped at 5 AI messages per free trip — [travelanywhere](https://www.travelanywhere.blog/blog/best-agentic-ai-travel-apps-2026-mindtrip-layla-stippl-stardrift-wanderlog-tested)
- **Stardrift — itinerary + interactive shareable map,** map as central organising tool; conversational brief → day-by-day plan → exportable map with calendar sync; retains standing filters (e.g. no red-eye flights) across sessions — [travelanywhere](https://www.travelanywhere.blog/blog/best-agentic-ai-travel-apps-2026-mindtrip-layla-stippl-stardrift-wanderlog-tested)
- **Stippl — panel-stack, not map.** Plan → packing automation → budget tracker → expense splitter → photo journal, as layered panels covering the full trip lifecycle — [travelanywhere](https://www.travelanywhere.blog/blog/best-agentic-ai-travel-apps-2026-mindtrip-layla-stippl-stardrift-wanderlog-tested)
- **Maps GPT** — prompt-to-custom-map generator; mapping is the entire product ("extreme" map centrality) — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- **iPlan.ai** — mobile-only form-first flow, day-by-day cards, generation in under a minute, map centrality low-to-moderate — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/); [monkeytravel](https://monkeytravel.app/blog/best-ai-trip-planners-2026-compared)
- Map presence is near-universal among the trip-planning apps surveyed: TripProf, Wanderlog, Lambus, Stippl, Tripsy, Tripomatic, Layla, Mindtrip, iPlan.ai, Wanderboat, Polarsteps, FindPenguins, Roadtrippers and Google Travel all have one — [tripprof 20-app comparison](https://tripprof.com/en/blog/best-trip-planning-apps-2026/)
- Cross-app pattern observed in hands-on testing: "Chat as default entry point" for Mindtrip, Layla and Stardrift; photos/maps/community itineraries embedded *within* the planning interface rather than as separate views, to reduce tab-switching — [travelanywhere](https://www.travelanywhere.blog/blog/best-agentic-ai-travel-apps-2026-mindtrip-layla-stippl-stardrift-wanderlog-tested)

### Inferences
- The AI-native apps inherited the chatbot UI wholesale and treat the map as a visual garnish; the map-as-workspace model (drag-to-reorder pins, routing) belongs to the pre-AI itinerary apps (Wanderlog, Tripomatic, Roadtrippers). Nobody in the surveyed set has a genuinely map-native *agentic* editor — the direct-manipulation editing and the AI live in different products.
- Editing is the weakest shared link: in chat-first apps the only way to change a plan is to re-prompt, which means every revision costs a round-trip and the user never gets a stable, directly-manipulable object. That is an unoccupied position.
- "Day-by-day card list" is the universal output artefact. An app that differentiates on output *form* (e.g. time-geography, routing-aware, or a non-linear canvas) would look unlike every entrant here.

### Gaps
- No vendor-published screen-structure documentation was obtained; all UI descriptions above come from third-party review roundups, and the two most detailed sources disagree on how central Mindtrip's map is.
- Could not verify current Layla UI post-Expedia acquisition (whether the app has been re-skinned or merged into Expedia surfaces).

## Monetization: subscription price points, free-tier limits, affiliate commissions, B2B/white-label

### Takeaway
Direct consumer subscriptions in this category cluster tightly at roughly $40–50/year, and the best-funded players have no published consumer price at all — Mindtrip's revenue is booking commission, GuideGeek's is white-label DMO licensing. The pattern is that consumer AI planning is being given away and monetised somewhere else.

### Cited Findings
- **Mindtrip:** free for individual travellers (core itinerary planning and in-chat booking); monetises per booking via Priceline, Viator and Sabre partnerships; enterprise/travel-pro pricing is contact-based with no published rate card; a Creator tier at $19/month for monetising guides and advanced analytics is reported — [TeachUsTechnology](https://teachustechnology.com/mindtrip-ai-trip-planner-with-in-chat-booking/); [aipresso pricing roundup](https://aipresso.com/reviews/best-ai-travel-planners); [monkeyeatingmango](https://monkeyeatingmango.com/blog/mindtrip-alternatives-2026/)
- Mindtrip booking stack timeline: in-chat Flights May 2026, Stays July 2026 — [monkeytravel comparison](https://monkeytravel.app/blog/best-ai-trip-planners-2026-compared)
- **Layla:** free tier with limits; premium $9.99/month or $49.99/year — [aipresso](https://aipresso.com/reviews/best-ai-travel-planners); [monkeytravel](https://monkeytravel.app/blog/best-ai-trip-planners-2026-compared)
- Layla also earns on bookings via partner handoff (live Skyscanner pricing; Expedia inventory post-acquisition) — [monkeytravel](https://monkeytravel.app/blog/best-ai-trip-planners-2026-compared); [aipresso](https://aipresso.com/reviews/best-ai-travel-planners)
- **Wanderlog:** free tier capped at 5 AI messages per trip; Pro $39.99/year (US App Store, August 2026); offline access is paid-only — [travelanywhere](https://www.travelanywhere.blog/blog/best-agentic-ai-travel-apps-2026-mindtrip-layla-stippl-stardrift-wanderlog-tested); [tripprof](https://tripprof.com/en/blog/best-trip-planning-apps-2026/)
- **Wonderplan:** core planning free with no signup; sources conflict on whether a paid tier exists — one reports Premium at $49.99/year (offline PDF, advanced AI, live multi-user editing), another reports "complete itinerary + PDF export" free with no paid tier "for now" — [monkeytravel Wonderplan review](https://monkeytravel.app/blog/wonderplan-review-2026); contradicted by [aipresso](https://aipresso.com/reviews/best-ai-travel-planners)
- **Vacay:** free Personal (unlimited standard chatbot, chat history, destination guides); Premium $9.99/month (advanced models, saved itineraries, priority support); Professional $49/month (white-label tools for travel advisors) — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- **GuideGeek:** free to consumers; revenue comes from building custom white-label versions for DMOs/tourism boards — [yespress](https://yespress.io/matador-network); [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/306980/20240731/guidegeek-pioneers-custom-ai-for-the-travel-industry.htm)
- **Stippl:** free basic, €24.99/year Pro (adds offline) — [travelanywhere](https://www.travelanywhere.blog/blog/best-agentic-ai-travel-apps-2026-mindtrip-layla-stippl-stardrift-wanderlog-tested)
- **Stardrift:** free, "no caps or watermarks" — [travelanywhere](https://www.travelanywhere.blog/blog/best-agentic-ai-travel-apps-2026-mindtrip-layla-stippl-stardrift-wanderlog-tested)
- **iPlan.ai:** free download, $3.99 per AI generation — [monkeytravel](https://monkeytravel.app/blog/best-ai-trip-planners-2026-compared)
- **Trip Planner AI:** free plan plus a Pro upgrade; links out to Skyscanner, Booking.com, GetYourGuide (affiliate) — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- Category-wide pricing shape across 20 trip-planning apps: freemium dominant, annual subscriptions typically $40–60/year, with pay-per-trip emerging (Lambus €9.99, TripProf from €3.99); offline access, advanced collaboration and AI automation are the standard paywalled features — [tripprof](https://tripprof.com/en/blog/best-trip-planning-apps-2026/)
- "Most dedicated planners here are free with no published paid tier" but rely on booking partnerships for revenue rather than direct user fees — [aipresso](https://aipresso.com/reviews/best-ai-travel-planners)
- None of the dedicated planners is a true booking agent; all provide affiliate links only — with Mindtrip the exception claimed on in-chat booking — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/); [TeachUsTechnology](https://teachustechnology.com/mindtrip-ai-trip-planner-with-in-chat-booking/)

### Inferences
- $49/year is the de facto ceiling that the category has established for consumer AI trip planning. An indie app pricing above it is fighting a reference point set by venture-subsidised free tiers.
- The paywall lines are nearly identical everywhere: offline access, unlimited AI generations, multi-user editing, export. Those are therefore commoditised as *paid* features — which also means a free app that includes them is structurally differentiated against the incumbents' free tiers.
- Two viable non-subscription models are proven in this set: booking commission at scale (needs inventory deals — Sabre/Priceline/Viator — out of reach for an indie) and white-label B2B to DMOs (GuideGeek: 70+ tourism boards, 1.5M consumer users as a funnel/credibility layer).

### Gaps
- No disclosed revenue figures for Mindtrip, Wanderboat, or Wonderplan. A third-party estimate puts Layla ARR at ~$2.8M, but getlatka estimates are modelled, not reported — [getlatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/layla.ai).
- Commission rates on any of the booking partnerships are not disclosed anywhere I found.
- Wonderplan's paid tier is unresolved (see conflict above).

## Which ingest external content (links, social posts, screenshots, email confirmations), and how prominent

### Takeaway
Content ingestion is the single most under-built capability in the AI-native cohort: Mindtrip's "Start Anywhere" (link/screenshot/social post → itinerary) and Trip Planner AI's Instagram/TikTok saving are the only AI-native examples found, while email-confirmation parsing remains the property of the pre-AI organiser apps (TripIt, Wanderlog, Google Travel). Nobody has combined social/screenshot ingestion with booking-email parsing in one product.

### Cited Findings
- **Mindtrip "Start Anywhere"** converts "a link, screenshot, or social post" into a working itinerary; no email-receipt ingestion mentioned in that review — [TeachUsTechnology](https://teachustechnology.com/mindtrip-ai-trip-planner-with-in-chat-booking/)
- Mindtrip separately launched a "receipt function" alongside a funding announcement — PhocusWire headline "AI trip planner Mindtrip receives funding, launches receipt function"; the article body returned HTTP 403 and could not be read, so the feature's mechanics are unverified — [PhocusWire (403)](https://www.phocuswire.com/ai-travel-planner-mindtrip-receipts-funding)
- **Trip Planner AI** "accepts social media links and saved spots" — saving Instagram/TikTok spots and stitching them into optimised routes is its stated core — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- **Layla:** natural-language input only; "no explicit social or screenshot support mentioned" — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- **Wanderboat:** "destination and vibe descriptions only" — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- **Wonderplan:** form inputs only (destination, budget, dates, interests) — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- **GuideGeek:** natural-language queries; no link or social import evident — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- **Email/booking-confirmation parsing:** TripIt pioneered forwarding to an `@tripit.com` address and "built its whole product on it"; TripProf offers a "trip inbox"; Wanderlog, Tripsy and Polarsteps support Gmail forwarding — described as a capability "where few competitors match depth" — [tripprof](https://tripprof.com/en/blog/best-trip-planning-apps-2026/)
- Wanderlog ingests booking confirmations via Gmail forwarding or a trip-specific email address — [travelanywhere](https://www.travelanywhere.blog/blog/best-agentic-ai-travel-apps-2026-mindtrip-layla-stippl-stardrift-wanderlog-tested)
- **Google Travel** auto-syncs bookings from email (Gmail parsing) and layers Gemini prompts on top — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- Roundup's own gap analysis: "Only Trip Planner AI explicitly documents link/screenshot imports from Instagram/TikTok"; "No tool explicitly advertises URL ingestion for restaurant/attraction databases" — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)

### Inferences
- Mindtrip treats "Start Anywhere" as a headline differentiator, which suggests ingestion is a recognised wedge rather than an ignored one — but it is a wedge currently held by exactly one well-funded player and one apparently-dead one. That is an unusually thin field for a capability that matches how people actually collect travel ideas (saved reels, screenshots, group-chat links).
- The clean separation between "AI planners that take prose" and "organiser apps that take email" is a structural seam. Nothing surveyed owns the full inbound pipeline: social saves → screenshots → links → booking emails → one editable plan.

### Gaps
- Mindtrip's receipt/email feature could not be characterised (source blocked). It is unclear whether it parses booking confirmation emails, scans paper receipts for expenses, or something else.
- No information on whether Layla post-Expedia gained Expedia booking-email ingestion.

## Stated differentiators, in each company's own or closest-reported words

### Takeaway
The claimed differentiators divide into booking depth (Mindtrip: real in-chat transactions), personalisation framing (Layla: "identity-first" planning), discovery/local content (Wanderboat, GuideGeek), and price (Wonderplan, Stardrift: free). Almost nobody claims a differentiated *editing* or *organising* experience.

### Cited Findings
- **Mindtrip:** "the only major tool in this category with genuine in-chat booking across flights, hotels, dining, and experiences", combined with converting existing content into plans rather than starting from a blank chat — [TeachUsTechnology](https://teachustechnology.com/mindtrip-ai-trip-planner-with-in-chat-booking/)
- Mindtrip positioning per roundup: "one polished tool for the whole trip" — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)

### Inferences
- Layla's "identity-first" framing and Wanderboat's "deeply personalised" framing are the same claim in different words, and neither is verifiable by a user in the first session — which is why both end up competing on output speed and visual polish instead.
- The differentiators that are actually defensible in this set are the ones backed by assets an indie cannot copy: inventory contracts (Mindtrip), a 50M-follower media brand (Layla/Beautiful Destinations), a publisher content library plus DMO relationships (GuideGeek), a review corpus (TripAdvisor). ## Shut downs, pivots, acquisitions and the stated reasons

### Takeaway
The category has already consolidated once: Layla bought Roam Around (Feb 2024) and then Expedia bought Layla (Jul 2026), while Tripnotes, Hipmunk and Google Trips are outright dead. Skift's blunt framing is that "every consumer travel startup of the last decade pivoted to B2B, got acqui-hired, or shut down."

### Cited Findings
- **Layla → Expedia Group, 31 July 2026**, terms undisclosed; Layla continues to operate, team works alongside Expedia.com, Hotels.com and Vrbo; stated rationale is to combine Layla's planning/personalisation with Expedia's supply, first-party data and booking infrastructure — [Expedia IR](https://ir.expediagroup.com/news-and-events/news/news-details/2026/Expedia-Group-acquires-Layla-accelerating-its-AI-powered-trip-planning-and-booking-strategy/default.aspx); [Skift exclusive](https://skift.com/2026/07/31/expedia-acquired-ai-trip-planner-layla-exclusive/)

- **Trip Planner AI → acquired by Layla and closed**, per search summary of its Crunchbase profile; no date and no press confirmation found — [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/trip-planner-ai) — weakly sourced, flagged in Gaps

- **Hipmunk — dead.** Described by Skift as "the best-loved interface in travel search", acquired by SAP Concur and "switched off just over three years later"; Skift's lesson is that superior UX alone provided no structural competitive advantage — [Skift, 30 July 2026](https://skift.com/2026/07/30/dont-start-a-travel-startup/)

## Common patterns across all of them — where nobody is differentiated

### Takeaway
The category has converged on one product: type a prose brief into a chatbot, receive a day-by-day card list in under a minute, look at a supporting map, click out to a partner to book, and pay roughly $40–50/year if you want offline access or unlimited generations. The shared blind spots are editing, post-booking life, and inbound content.

### Cited Findings
- **Chat is the default entry point** for the AI-native cohort — Mindtrip, Layla, Stardrift all open on conversational input; GuideGeek and Vacay are chat-only — [travelanywhere](https://www.travelanywhere.blog/blog/best-agentic-ai-travel-apps-2026-mindtrip-layla-stippl-stardrift-wanderlog-tested); [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- **Day-by-day card list is the universal output.** Layla, Wonderplan, iPlan.ai, MonkeyTravel, Stardrift all present the plan as day-grouped cards — [monkeytravel](https://monkeytravel.app/blog/best-ai-trip-planners-2026-compared); [travelanywhere](https://www.travelanywhere.blog/blog/best-agentic-ai-travel-apps-2026-mindtrip-layla-stippl-stardrift-wanderlog-tested)
- **Speed is a shared pitch:** Wonderplan ~60 seconds, iPlan.ai "under one minute" — [monkeytravel Wonderplan review](https://monkeytravel.app/blog/wonderplan-review-2026); [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- **Booking is a handoff, not a transaction.** "None are true booking agents; all provide affiliate links only"; four of five apps in hands-on testing "transition to external booking sites rather than containing the full transaction — a deliberate architectural choice reflecting inventory access and payment liability constraints" — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/); [travelanywhere](https://www.travelanywhere.blog/blog/best-agentic-ai-travel-apps-2026-mindtrip-layla-stippl-stardrift-wanderlog-tested)
- **A map exists almost everywhere but is rarely the workspace.** 14 of the 20 apps compared have maps; among the AI-native planners the map is "secondary (supportive, not central)" or "moderate" — [tripprof](https://tripprof.com/en/blog/best-trip-planning-apps-2026/); [travelanywhere](https://www.travelanywhere.blog/blog/best-agentic-ai-travel-apps-2026-mindtrip-layla-stippl-stardrift-wanderlog-tested); [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- **Identical paywall lines:** offline access, advanced collaboration and AI automation are the standard premium features; annual subscriptions cluster at $40–60/year — [tripprof](https://tripprof.com/en/blog/best-trip-planning-apps-2026/)
- **Free-tier limiting is done by metering AI**, not by crippling the plan: Wanderlog 5 AI messages per free trip; iPlan.ai $3.99 per AI generation; Wonderplan "credit-based system for heavy use" — [travelanywhere](https://www.travelanywhere.blog/blog/best-agentic-ai-travel-apps-2026-mindtrip-layla-stippl-stardrift-wanderlog-tested); [monkeytravel](https://monkeytravel.app/blog/best-ai-trip-planners-2026-compared); [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- **Personalisation is the universal claim.** Layla ("identity-first", 1,400 micro-segments), Wanderboat ("deeply personalised"), Vacay ("personalisation by travel style"), Wonderplan (interests/budget), JourneAI ("personalization focus") all assert it — [Layla release](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/layla-surpasses-1-billion-trips-165400507.html); [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/)
- **Shared coverage gap in the AI-native cohort:** no social/screenshot ingestion except Mindtrip and Trip Planner AI; no advertised URL ingestion; email/calendar parsing largely absent from the AI planners and present only in the older organiser apps — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/); [tripprof](https://tripprof.com/en/blog/best-trip-planning-apps-2026/)
- **Shared coverage gap in post-booking/trip-life features:** document storage appears in only 5 of 20 apps (TripProf, Lambus, TripIt, Tripsy, Polarsteps); packing checklists in only 4 (TripProf, Stippl, PackPoint, iPlan.ai); expense splitting is siloed in dedicated apps rather than integrated into planners, with TripProf and Lambus the rare exceptions — [tripprof](https://tripprof.com/en/blog/best-trip-planning-apps-2026/)
- **No single tool covers the whole workflow.** "All tools address three workflow phases: research, planning structure, and booking coordination. None dominate all three — specialization matters more than comprehensiveness for user satisfaction." — [monkeytravel](https://monkeytravel.app/blog/best-ai-trip-planners-2026-compared)
- **Platform convergence:** web plus iOS is the norm; Android presence is inconsistent (iPlan.ai is mobile-only; Wonderplan, Stardrift, Maps GPT, Trip Planner AI, Vacay, Copilot2trip are web-only; GuideGeek is messaging-only) — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/best-ai-tools/best-ai-travel-planners/); [monkeytravel](https://monkeytravel.app/blog/best-ai-trip-planners-2026-compared)

### Inferences
Unoccupied positions that follow directly from the convergence above:
- **Editing as the product.** Every chat-first app makes revision a re-prompt. A direct-manipulation plan object (drag, reorder, lock, constrain) with AI as an assistant rather than the only interface is the clearest open seam — the map-native editors (Wanderlog) have the manipulation but treat AI as a bolt-on, and the AI-natives have the AI but no manipulable object.
- **Inbound content pipeline.** Only Mindtrip claims link/screenshot/social ingestion, and no surveyed app combines that with booking-email parsing. Owning the full inbound path (saved reels → screenshots → links → confirmation emails → one plan) is contested by exactly one well-funded player.
- **The trip itself.** The category optimises the pre-booking funnel because that is where commission lives. Document storage, packing, expenses and in-trip adaptation are absent from nearly every AI planner — and these are the features that create return visits, which is precisely what a subscription (rather than commission) business needs.
- **Not competing on booking.** Mindtrip's in-chat booking rests on Sabre/Priceline/Viator contracts and payment liability an indie cannot assume. The strategically sound indie move is to be deliberately booking-agnostic, which also removes the incentive conflict that shapes every competitor's UI.
- **Pricing.** With the reference price at $39.99–$49.99/year and offline/unlimited-AI as the standard paywall, an indie either prices under it or gives those away and charges for something nobody else charges for.

### Gaps
- "Common patterns" above are synthesised from third-party roundups rather than first-hand use of each app; I could not independently verify screen structures. A hands-on pass on Mindtrip, Wanderboat and Wonderplan would firm this up considerably.
- No retention or engagement data (D30, repeat-trip rate) was found for any app in the set, so the claim that the category has a retention problem is an inference from feature coverage and exit patterns, not a measured fact.
- Could not establish current app-store ranking or download velocity for any competitor; App Annie/Sensor Tower-class data was not accessible.
